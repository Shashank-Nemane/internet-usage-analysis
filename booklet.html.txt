        <span class="label">Abstract :</span><br>
        University students rely heavily on the internet for daily academic and personal activities, primarily spanning four key behavioral dimensions: education, entertainment, social media, and communication. However, unstructured screen time often leads to digital fatigue and reduced academic productivity. This mini-project, <b>NetPulse: Student Internet Usage Analytics Platform</b>, aims to statistically model and analyze daily internet usage patterns across a cohort of 40 university students to identify behavioral trends, usage consistency, and distributional anomalies. Using a real-time client-side mathematical engine, the platform computes seven core descriptive statistical metrics for each usage category: Mean, Median, Mode, Range, Standard Deviation, Coefficient of Variation (CV%), and Skewness. While measures of central tendency establish baseline peer averages, the Coefficient of Variation normalizes dispersion across categories to identify which student habits are most consistent versus highly variable. Furthermore, Fisher-Pearson Skewness quantifies distributional asymmetry to distinguish between light-user clusters and heavy binge-usage outliers. To translate raw statistical findings into actionable insights, the web application features an interactive 24-hour-capped Habit Simulator. Instead of relying solely on potentially unhealthy peer averages, the simulator evaluates individual student inputs against both the cohort mean and ideal productivity benchmarks (&ge; 4.0 hrs/day for education, &le; 1.5 hrs/day for entertainment, and &le; 1.0 hr/day for social media), generating a dynamic Personal Action Plan and a 0–100 Digital Wellness Score. Hosted on GitHub Pages with local state persistence, the platform provides an intuitive, data-driven framework for student digital wellness.
